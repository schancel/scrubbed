Learning to Bake Sourdough at Home

Sourdough bread relies on a naturally fermented starter rather than commercial yeast, which gives the finished loaf its distinctive tang and chewy crumb structure that many home bakers spend years perfecting.

Feeding your starter consistently, at the same time each day, keeps the wild yeast and bacteria culture active and predictable, which in turn makes the dough's rise far easier to plan around a normal schedule.

Once the dough has proofed, a hot Dutch oven traps steam during the first few minutes of baking, producing the crackling crust that distinguishes a good sourdough loaf from an ordinary sandwich bread.
